  **(a) The condition.** Confirmed at `mp/extmod/modlwip.c:748-812`: an accepted socket is non-blocking
  (`extmod/asyncio/stream.py:172`, `setblocking(False)`), so with `tcp_sndbuf() == 0` the write returns `EAGAIN`
  (`:763-767`) and never loops; only with `available > 0` does `write_len = MIN(available, len)` reach the 200 × 50 ms
  `ERR_MEM` retry (`:800-812`), inside one C call, and the watchdog is fed only from the asyncio supervisor loop
  (`src/system_service.py:249`), so a full 10 s exceeds the 8,388 ms cap. ACKs arriving during the wait are still
  processed (lwIP runs from the PendSV dispatch, `ports/rp2/mpnetworkport.c:95`, and the loop releases the lock
  around `mp_hal_delay_ms()`), so a *live* peer ends the wait within its RTT: the 10 s block needs the arena held by
  peers that stopped ACKing (a phone leaving range mid-load) — the realistic case. But `ERR_MEM` with `available > 0`
  has **three** sources, not one: (1) `mem_malloc()` failing in the `MEM_SIZE` arena (`lwip/src/core/pbuf.c:275-286`);
  (2) the global `MEMP_TCP_SEG` pool empty (`tcp_create_segment()`, `lwip/src/core/tcp_out.c:171` →
  `memerr`); (3) the per-pcb queue limit (`tcp_write_checks()` `:336`, phase 3 `:662`), reached while `snd_buf > 0`
  whenever the queued pbufs average under `TCP_SND_BUF / TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` = 200 B. A `MEM_SIZE`-only proof closes (1)
  only.

  **The author's arithmetic undercounts twice.**
  - *Holders are `MEMP_NUM_TCP_PCB` (9), not `max_connections` (6).* A closed connection keeps its unsent/unacked
    segments: modlwip's close runs `tcp_close()` (FIN queued behind the data) and arms an abort only after
    `MICROPY_PY_LWIP_TCP_CLOSE_TIMEOUT_MS` = 10 s (`modlwip.c:62-64, 1686-1694`); the slot is freed at close
    (SPEC H.7 `:4561-4568` already says closing pcbs outlive their slot). A dead client's data therefore sits in the
    arena for the 5 s per-call write timeout plus up to 10 s after close, while a new arrival takes the slot. Every pcb
    of the pool can hold send data at once.
  - *Oversize space is not only in the last pbuf.* `tcp_pbuf_prealloc()` allocates `min(mss_local, align(len +
    TCP_OVERSIZE))` whenever the pcb has unacked data and Nagle is on (`tcp_out.c:245-263`); once the segment is sent
    the pcb forgets its oversize (`:1414-1418`, `unsent_oversize = 0`) but the pbuf keeps its allocation until ACKed
    (no trim on send; `pbuf_realloc()` only in `tcp_split_unsent_seg()` `:936`). Under normal Nagle a partial segment
    leaves only when nothing is in flight, so the author's "+ `TCP_MSS` − 1" holds; but Nagle is bypassed under fast
    recovery (`TF_INFR`, 3 dup ACKs) and after an `ERR_MEM` (`TF_NAGLEMEMERR`) (`lwip/src/include/lwip/priv/tcp_priv.h:100-105`),
    and then every 256 B write leaves as its own segment in an 800 B allocation. The strict per-pcb bound is
    `TCP_SND_QUEUELEN × (8 + 16 + 76 + TCP_MSS)`.

  Computed (u16 `mem_size_t`; `MEM_SIZE > 64000` switches it to u32 and `SIZEOF_STRUCT_MEM` to 12, `lwip/src/include/lwip/mem.h:63-69`):

  | set | `TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` | per pcb, Nagle-normal (`SND_BUF + QL×103 + MSS−1`) | × 9 + UDP 3,075 | per pcb, strict (`QL × 900`) | × 9 + UDP |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | HEAD (6400) | 32 | 10,495 | 97,530 (u32: 98,702) | 28,800 | 262,275 |
  | 1600 | 8 | 3,223 | 32,082 | 7,200 | 67,875 (u32: 68,183) |
  | 1600 + `TCP_NODELAY` (no oversize, `tcp_out.c:257-263`) | 8 | 2,424 (`SND_BUF + QL×103`) | 24,891 | = Nagle column, strict | 24,891 |

  The author's "~63 KB" is 6 × 10,495 + 3,075 = 66,045 (arena demand of six holders under the Nagle-normal model);
  HEAD's true Nagle-normal demand is 97.5 KB, strict 262 KB, against 12,000. The segment pool needs
  `MEMP_NUM_TCP_SEG ≥ 9 × TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` for source (2): 288 at HEAD, 72 at 1600 (48 shipped).

  **(b) Reachable by this webserver — yes, at HEAD with two dead clients.** Every body write is ≤ `chunk_bytes`
  256 B (`src/asy_webserver_service.py:108, 186, 578, 666`), the header block one joined write (`:268-279`); microdot's

  queues `min(TCP_SND_BUF, response bytes)` unacknowledged. The staged `app.js` is ~16 KB gzipped (the seven

  **(c) Heap cost — the author's premise holds, the figures do not.** `ram_heap` is a static array

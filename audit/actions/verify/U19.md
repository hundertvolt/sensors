- V.U19.Q0 | A.U19.22 / A.U14.30 / OR112.a sizing | ADD | evidence below | the decided set (`TCP_SND_BUF` 1600, derived
  `TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` 8, `MEMP_NUM_TCP_SEG` 72, `MEM_SIZE` 24,900) closes the arena source of modlwip's 10 s `ERR_MEM`
  loop but not the per-pcb queue-length source, under either variant; corrections in V.U19.Q1 and V.U19.30.

  **Verified from source (not from the lead's model).**
  - *Queue checks.* `tcp_write_checks()` returns `ERR_MEM` when `pcb->snd_queuelen >= TCP_SND_QUEUELEN`
    (`lwip/src/core/tcp_out.c:336-342`) — independent of `snd_buf`; phase 2 (chain onto the last unsent segment) adds
    `pbuf_clen()` with no check (`:527-553`); phase 3 fails the whole write when `queuelen > TCP_SND_QUEUELEN`
    (`:662-667`). modlwip writes `write_len = MIN(available, len)` (`extmod/modlwip.c:792`) and loops 200 × 50 ms on
    `ERR_MEM` (`:800-812`); an accepted socket is non-blocking, so the loop is reached only with `available > 0`.
  - *ACKs free whole segments only*: `tcp_free_acked_segments()` frees a segment only when fully acked and adds its
    `len` to `recv_acked` (`tcp_in.c:1110-1127`); `snd_buf += recv_acked` (`:1322`). A partial ACK frees nothing — so
    no adversarial "1 byte at a time" freeing exists; frees arrive in whole-segment amounts.
  - *Initial cwnd* of an accepted pcb: `LWIP_TCP_CALC_INITIAL_CWND(mss)` = min(4·mss, max(2·mss, 4380)) = 3,200 at
    MSS 800 (`tcp_in.c:69, 976`) ≥ `TCP_SND_BUF` 1600, so cwnd never limits a first flight.
  - *mss_local* = min(`pcb->mss`, `snd_wnd_max / 2`) (`tcp_out.c:420-421`); `pcb->mss` = min(peer MSS option, 800)
    (`tcp_in.c:1956-1959`). A peer advertising MSS 536, or a window below 1,600 B, gives segments under 800 B.
  - *Nagle*: `tcp_do_output_nagle()` holds the last partial unsent segment while anything is unacked, unless
    `TF_NODELAY`/`TF_INFR`, more than one unsent segment, `snd_buf == 0` or the queue is full
    (`lwip/src/include/lwip/priv/tcp_priv.h:100-105`); `tcp_output()` applies it per segment (`tcp_out.c:1354-1363`).
    With Nagle on, later writes chain onto that held segment (phase 2), and a write straddling its end becomes two
    pbufs (phase-2 remainder + phase-3 new segment).
  - *Mid-stream short writes do happen, routinely*: POLLOUT is reported whenever `tcp_sndbuf() > 0`
    (`modlwip.c:1639`); `Stream.write()` writes at once and keeps the unwritten remainder (`extmod/asyncio/stream.py:66-74`);
    `drain()` writes the remainder at the next POLLOUT as `s.write(mv[off:])` (`:81-88`) — so every 256 B piece that
    meets `snd_buf < 256` becomes two `tcp_write()` calls. And JSON pieces are *at most* `chunk_bytes`, not equal to
    it: `_PieceWriter.add()` flushes before a fragment that would overflow (`src/asy_webserver_service.py:141-143`).
    A.U14.30's "every body write is `chunk_bytes` except the last" is true of the Python pieces, false of the
    `tcp_write()` calls that the queue limit counts.
  - *Smallest header block* the webserver writes (the proxy joins the head into one write, `:268-279`; Microdot's
    status line + headers, `ext/microdot.py:665-681`; `Connection: close` added by `_mark_connection_close()` `:799`):
    **104 B** (a 200 JSON response with a one-digit `Content-Length`: 17 + 47 + 19 + 19 + 2); 105 B for a shaped
    error or a static file (`Content-Type: text/css` 24 + `Content-Encoding: gzip` 24); a redirect 112 B; A.U19.06's
    `Cache-Control: no-cache` adds 25 B to static heads. So no real head falls in the 1-63 B window the lead's model
    flagged at MSS 800 (it does fall in the 1-319 B window at MSS 536).

  **Independent model** (`al/vU19/lwsim.py`: phases 2/3 and both queue checks, per-segment Nagle, whole-segment ACK
  frees, `write_len = min(available, len)`, `Stream` remainder drain, cwnd 3,200; head 104-140 B, then static 256 B
  reads or `_PieceWriter`-shaped JSON pieces; ACK policy per run: one segment, two segments, or a random prefix;
  app/ACK interleaving random). Metric: the smallest `TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` for which no write in the run hits the queue
  check with `snd_buf > 0` (= a state where a peer vanishing leaves modlwip in its 10 s loop). Two seeds, 1,500 and
  3,000 runs per cell, median / p99 / max:

  | peer | `TCP_OVERSIZE 0` (Nagle on) | `TCP_NODELAY` |
  |---|---|---|
  | MSS 800, large window, ACK every segment | 8 / 10 / 10 | 9 / 25 / **29** |
  | MSS 800, large window, ACK every 2nd segment | 8 / 10 / 10 | 7 / 12 / 13 |
  | MSS 800, large window, random cumulative ACKs | 8 / 10 / 10 | 7 / 10 / 11 |
  | MSS 536 (any ACK policy) | 9 / 11 / 11 | 10 / 25 / 27 |

  At QL 8 the check is reached with room in 23-52 % of Nagle runs and 4-24 % of NODELAY runs (`sweep.py`). The lead's
  model is **confirmed** on its first point (`TCP_OVERSIZE 0` is not equivalent to `TCP_NODELAY`; with Nagle the
  queue fills with room while writes chain behind unacked data) and **refuted** on two: (i) "`TCP_NODELAY` at QL 8
  never stalls on the realistic pattern" — it does, because ACK clocking splits pieces into short writes mid-stream
  (trace: after per-segment ACKs the queue holds 256, 256, 256, 256, 64, 192, 64, 192 = 1,536 B in 8 pbufs, and the
  next 56 B write gets `ERR_MEM` with 64 B of room); (ii) "QL 10 is stall-free for both" — NODELAY needs up to 29
  when the peer ACKs every segment (Linux/Android receivers do so in quick-ACK mode at connection start, which covers
  most of a short response). Mechanism: with NODELAY each write is its own segment, frees come one write at a time,
  and the partial-write split points (`lastack + TCP_SND_BUF`, with 1600 ≡ 64 mod 256) fall on two 64 B lattices
  offset by the head length, so writes shrink to 20-40 B; with Nagle, segments fill to `mss_local` and frees come in
  ~800 B steps, so the split points stay aligned. Model limits: no RTO/fast-recovery, fixed peer window, no
  delayed-ACK timer; a sample, not a proof — for JSON bodies (piece sizes vary) no finite QL is provably stall-free.

  **What each choice costs** (bound per A.U14.30: `MEM_SIZE ≥ 9 × (1600 + QL × 103) + 3,075`, `MEMP_NUM_TCP_SEG ≥
  9 × QL`; GC heap 1:1 for `MEM_SIZE`, 16 B per segment slot, SPEC B.14.2 `:1351, 1354`; Δ against the decided
  24,900 / 72):

  | QL | `MEM_SIZE` | SEG | Δ GC heap | model result |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 8 (decided) | 24,900 | 72 | 0 | both variants reach the stall |
  | 10 | 26,745 | 90 | +2,133 B | Nagle clean for ordinary peers (MSS 800) |
  | 11 | 27,672 | 99 | +3,204 B | Nagle clean at MSS 536; NODELAY clean with delayed/random ACKs |
  | 13 | 29,526 | 117 | +5,346 B | Nagle clean in every modelled case |
  | 15 | 31,380 | 135 | +7,488 B | NODELAY clean except per-segment ACKs |
  | 29 | 44,358 | 261 | +22,482 B | NODELAY clean in every modelled case |
  | 32 | 47,139 | 288 | +25,695 B | margin over the NODELAY maximum |

  Add 1,071 B (9 × (103 + 16)) to any row for the persist-timer split: `tcp_split_unsent_seg()` adds one pbuf and one
  segment past the queue limit ("length now may exceed TCP_SND_QUEUELEN", `tcp_out.c:958-963`), reached from
  `tcp_slowtmr()` when a small peer window cuts the head segment (`tcp.c:1257-1259`); it takes arena and segment-pool
  room another pcb's write may need. `TCP_SND_QUEUELEN` is settable (`opt.h:1389-1391`, `#if !defined`); init.c's
  checks stay met (`TCP_SNDQUEUELOWAT` = max(QL/2, 5) < QL for QL ≥ 6, `init.c:321-322`; QL ≥ 2·(1600/800)).
  **Recommendation (for the lead's owner question):** keep `TCP_NODELAY` (decided, V.U19.Q1: its race is harmless on
  the RP2040) only with QL ≥ 29 (+22.5 KB, near the 13.3 KB OR112.a saved); or `TCP_OVERSIZE 0` with QL 13

  "the arena bound holds for every peer", which holds; the queue premise was never put — this is a new owner question,
  not a correction of OR112.a. Either way phase C drives the queue limit to its edge (OR112.a already lists it).

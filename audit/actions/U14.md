## Open points
1. **lwIP send memory: which fix for the 10 s write stall?** (A.U14.30; asked 2026-09-30, AC_NOTES 12) Nine TCP pcbs (six open connections plus
   three closing, which keep their data up to 10 s) can queue ~97.5 KB of outbound pbufs against a 12,000 B
   `MEM_SIZE`; when the arena, the 48-slot segment pool or a pcb's queue limit runs out while the socket still
   reports room, modlwip retries the write for up to 10 s inside one call, past the 8.4 s watchdog. Two clients
   vanishing mid page load are enough today.
   - (a) `TCP_SND_BUF` 1600 (lwIP's minimum, 2 × MSS), `MEM_SIZE` 32,100, `MEMP_NUM_TCP_SEG` 72 — the proof holds
     under Nagle; about 20.5 KB less GC heap (192.5 → ~172 KB); at most 1.6 KB in flight per connection, so the
     16 KB `app.js` needs ≥ 10 round trips. A peer forcing fast recovery can still strand 800 B per 256 B write
     (strict bound 67.9 KB), so the proof covers well-behaved peers only.
   - (b) As (a), plus `TCP_NODELAY` on every accepted socket (`_serve()`; supported at `extmod/modlwip.c:1527-1535`)
     and `MEM_SIZE` 24,900 — no oversized pbufs, so the bound is strict for every peer; about 13.3 KB less GC heap;
     every 256 B write leaves as its own segment (more packets per page, same 1.6 KB window). Recommended.
   - (c) Keep `TCP_SND_BUF` 6400 and size the arena for it — `MEM_SIZE` ≥ 98,700 (Nagle) — about 87 KB less GC
     heap, near half of it; page loads unchanged. Not practical.
   - (d) Keep today's sizing and accept a watchdog reboot when clients vanish mid-load — no proof; overrides your
     OR49.a rule "no crash, reboot, WDT reset" under connection load.

New facts: the curve is the FRAM hold defect, not CPU. A reset is one chunk write per logger (`asy_print_log.py:339-356`, C); a log block write holds 14 scheduler rounds under the FRAM and SPI locks (designfu, C), so 21 chunks x 2 copies x 14 = 588 held rounds; +3.5 s per reader / 588 = ~6 ms added per round per reader, i.e. a round of ~20 ms at three readers - the 16-22 ms designfu measured (C); idle 588 x ~2 ms + 42 holds x ~23 ms = ~2.2 s against 2.31-2.43 measured (I, a fit). Under one FIFO lock gather cannot shorten serialised holds, so the fan-out buys no time today and costs the heap burst (I); the concurrent reset was never timed on silicon (BACKLOG: "last measured ... on the one-after-another reset since replaced", C). After B(ii) the 15 s leg fails either way: sequential ~1-3 s if the CRC yield goes (4c), ~8 s loaded if it stays, where gather then overlaps the per-chunk CRC rounds outside the lock (I). Levels: integration (logger x FRAM chunk x webserver) and system (readers). Origin: pre-audit ruling, audit code.
To decide with B(ii) and 4c: whether the fan-out stays (sequential loop, no task per source, is the leaner form if 4c removes the yield), and whether writer starvation still needs accepting.

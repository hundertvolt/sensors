# The bench's open questions, researched (audit session, 2026-11-02)

Owner, 15:53: "Try to answer the other deep detail questions by proper research". Sources: the bench record
(`READING_20261029.md`), the pinned MicroPython v1.29.0 with its pico-sdk and cyw43-driver, the datasheets, the RP2040
bootrom source, and two twin runs. No hardware, no product code changed, nothing pushed.

## 1. Who wrote the FRAM log entries (answered)

Each chunk's CRC8 is seeded from its owner's name (`asy_fram_manager.py` `_owner_seed()`), so the seed that validates a
chunk names its owner. All eleven valid chunks of the 2026-10-28 dump resolve to one owner each, in construction order:
NEOPIXEL, WIFI, CFGMGR_WIFI, DNSSRV, NTP, CFGMGR_NTP, SYSTEM, CFGMGR_SYSTEM, SCD30, SGP40, CFGMGR_SGP40.
- WIFI, W81 twice: "WLAN connect attempt ended without a verdict" (no IP within the attempt; `asy_wifi_service.py:636`).
- CFGMGR_WIFI, E21 once: a refused config write (not an object, an unknown key, or a type or range error;
  `asy_config_manager.py:630-640`).
- SGP40, W34 once: the VOC backup's age out of range (`asy_sgp40_driver.py:581`), expected after a long power-off.
None is a fault; all fit test runs against the board. No W29, as decision 3 item 4 states (the FRAM log is RAM-only).

## 2. The two SGP40 NACKs in the long runs (answered: the script's timing)

- The datasheet: "When the measurement is in progress, no communication with the sensor is possible and the sensor
  aborts the communication with a NACK condition"; `measure_raw_signal` takes 25 ms typical, 30 ms maximum (Table 8).
- `long_i2c.py` sets the read deadline as `now + 30` with `now` taken at the top of the pass, before that pass's BMP3XX
  and ISL29125 reads (about 2.3 ms), the every-tenth-pass register writes (up to about 5 ms each) and the SCD30 command
  or read (up to 5.4 ms with clock stretching). The measure command goes out after all of them, so in a slow pass the
  read lands 30 ms after the pass start but only about 13-25 ms after the command, before the measurement ends.
- Both NACKs (1 in 8,452 reads overall) are that coincidence. The firmware waits 100 ms (`_MEASURE_WAIT_MS`), so it is
  not affected; nothing to change.

## 3. The crc16 hazard results (not reproducible off silicon)

- `next_ok` and `clean_after` are one echo exchange right after a race or a field sweep, with no `clear()` first; the
  flash-tier test (`test_comm_hazards_are_serialised_refused_or_recovered`) asserts both true, so it fails on silicon in
  crc16 mode. `next_ok` failed at a different race point in each of the two runs (13:45 `clear_after_last_ack`, old
  driver; 15:10 `cancel_read_timeout_mid_train`, fixed driver); `clean_after` failed after the cmd sweep in both.
- The twin passes both CRC modes, and passes again with silicon's measured CRC cost emulated (440 us per byte in a
  scratch copy of the script; the run took 4 s longer, so the emulation took effect; the raw logs stay in the
  session's scratch, out of the evidence). The twin models wire time at the configured baud. So CRC slowness alone does not cause it: the cause is in something
  only silicon has (the DMA ring and its re-arm around `clear()`, the interpreter being slower everywhere, not only in
  the CRC). Not a root cause yet.
- Next step, in round 1: rerun the script in crc16 mode with both ends' logs and the driver counters (`rx_overruns`,
  discarded bytes, the drain counts) recorded at every failing exchange. Until then the plan's B6 row keeps it as
  knowledge, not a defect (rule 1: no reproduction).

## 4. Why a BOOTSEL round trip loses both reset-record regions (answered: the bootrom)

- The RP2040 bootrom's `reset_usb_boot()` (pico-bootrom-rp2040, `bootrom/bootrom_main.c`), which
  `machine.bootloader()` calls (`ports/rp2/modmachine.c:91-96`), writes `scratch[0]` and `scratch[1]` with its two
  arguments, then reboots through the watchdog-boot vector, which uses `scratch[4..7]` (datasheet 2.8.1.1).
- Region 0 (`scratch[0..3]`) loses its magic word and region 1 (`scratch[5..7]`) is overwritten, at the entry into
  BOOTSEL, before picotool is involved. So code 4 can never be read back through `machine.bootloader()`, by design of
  the bootrom. `scratch[2]` and `scratch[3]` are not written by `reset_usb_boot()`; whether they survive the whole
  round trip is not known (untested).

## 5. A Python-only way out of a LAN UDP storm (possible in principle, unproven)

- The stall: the CYW43 host-wake IRQ schedules `cyw43_poll` as a PendSV dispatch (`mpnetworkport.c:86-100`), and
  `cyw43_ll_process_packets()` loops until no frame is left (`cyw43_ll.c:1128`), so thread mode starves while frames
  keep coming. Soft callbacks still ran 8-12 times a second in the bench's storm.
- `wlan.disconnect()` from such a callback would take the lwIP lock, which suspends PendSV (`lwip_lock_acquire()`,
  `mpnetworkport.c:127-130`; `CYW43_THREAD_ENTER` is `MICROPY_PY_LWIP_ENTER`), and its ioctl processes data frames
  inline until the control reply arrives (`cyw43_do_ioctl()`, `cyw43_ll.c:1153-1182`), within a 500 ms timeout. So a
  starvation check in an existing soft-timer callback could disconnect the station and end the stall without a reboot.
- What it would not do: keep the device reachable (the storm resumes at the reconnect), or avoid a new supervisor beside
  the watchdog the owner chose. Whether the ioctl beats its 500 ms timeout under a real storm needs one bench run.
- Recommendation: not adopted unless the owner asks for it; the watchdog stays the backstop as decided.

## 6. The SCD30's 2.14 s rhythm at a 2 s interval (answered: undocumented)

- The datasheet and the interface description state the interval (2 s initial, stored in NVM) but no tolerance; the
  measured median 2,143 ms and maximum 2,250 ms are a property of this chip.
- The interface description also states clock stretching of up to 30 ms before any ACK and "a maximal clock stretching
  of 150 ms may occur once per day" (section 1.1). The long runs saw 5.4 ms. So the bench's error-free reads at a 1 ms
  I2C timeout do not make short timeouts safe: dev's 200 ms on bus 1 and B8's 1-200,000 us range stay justified.

## 7. `loop_lag_under_combined_load` over the mount (answered: wrong context)

It measures "the bench build's shipped main()" (its own header) and imports the generated `sensortask_dev`, which
imports `frozen_html`. That module is built only by `build_stage_dir()` (the website, frozen into the image), while
the bench staged with `stage_python_modules()`. It belongs to the flash and bench tiers on a firmware image
(`tests_hardware/flash/test_memory_stress.py`, `bench/test_memory_stress_bench.py`), so it runs in round 1.

## 8. The country code (answered, and the bench's reading corrected)

- `network.country()` only stores two characters in MicroPython's own `mod_network_country_code` and returns them
  (`extmod/modnetwork.c:71-144`). The bench's "readback" never asked the chip.
- The code reaches the chip only at `active(True)` (`network_cyw43.c:129-149`), and `cyw43_ll_wifi_on()` discards the
  country ioctl's result (`cyw43_ll.c:1856-1865`). Nothing reports an invalid code, so the firmware's own check is the
  only guard.
- Which regulatory domain the chip then applies is visible only from the air: round 1's item 8 can scan under "DE" and
  "XX" and list the channels seen.

## 9. The general call on bus 1 (answered)

Of the three chips on bus 1, only the SGP40's datasheet names the general call (`0x00 0x06`, "soft_reset": the SGP40
"will restart entering the idle mode"); the SCD30's interface description and the ISL29125 datasheet do not. The one
ACK on bus 1 was the SGP40, which then sat idle, heater off.

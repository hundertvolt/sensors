**5. Where the fix should start (I).** Dev has about 60 KB free at idle, in runs of at most about 2 KB, so /status's body must cost one response whatever the reader count. The smallest form is one built piece list shared by every /status reader in flight:
- It is built under the existing lock, and only when no reader still holds the current list.
- Each reader walks it by its own index and never drains the shared list; the last reader to finish frees it.
- A reader that arrives after the list is older than one send (about 1.4 s on silicon) waits for the sends in flight to end, then the list is rebuilt once.

The body then costs about 8.6 KB in 30 pieces at any reader count, plus about 3.6 KB per connection (MEM, twin). Six readers need about 30 KB of the 60, and the design keeps the exact `Content-Length` and the 500 for a failed build. The evidence is the bench's share hook: 0 of 117 failed at 3 readers. Its 3 failures in 101 at 6 readers came from rebuilding every 2 s while a slow send still held the previous list, which "rebuild only at zero readers" removes. If 30 placements still fail at 6, the next step is the same snapshot in one buffer allocated at boot. Part N's budget needs re-deriving from about 60 KB either way, and the ring's 8 KB of slack is a lever for the owner.

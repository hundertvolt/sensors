## Question for the owner (the NeoPixel overlay), in prose
The NeoPixel overlay task loses a shutdown cancel that lands as its five-minute refresh wait times out (`src/asy_neopixel_driver.py:82`): it catches the TimeoutError and loops, so the shutdown sequence waits on it forever and the watchdog resets the unit with the command not carried out (ResetReason 9). A fix is ready, red first and bitten (patch `S/u35/laneC/lc1/neopixel_alarm_form.patch`; its two SPEC rows are in `spec_both.patch`): the refresh is woken by a short alarm task that sets the overlay's flag after the period, and the overlay task awaits only its flag, so a cancel always reaches it. The refresh timing is unchanged, and it allocates less per wake (928-1,024 B against 1,504-1,600 B on the 64-bit Unix port, `meas_overlay_*.log`). But the overlay now wakes in the scheduler round its flag is set, where the old form woke two to three rounds later (wait_for's own runner chain), and that changes two frame sequences: at a boot or restart where both LED tasks start together, the overlay is written twice instead of once (the same value); and when a queued LED request polls at the very instant the previous ramp ends, the overlay's restore frame is now written between the two ramps instead of after the second one. For any other poll phase (a queued request polls every 50 ms, so on a device its phase is arbitrary) the old form already wrote it between the ramps, and both forms give identical frames (`probe_np5_*.log`, `probe_np6_*.log`). Three test files pin the old sequences and would change with it: the two notification fault tests expect exactly two start frames, and `tests/test_neopixel_wifi_integration.py`'s `_FlashSleeps` speeds up every `asyncio.sleep_ms`, now also the 300 s refresh alarm (the old timeout slept through core's own sleep), so two of its tests fail until that helper leaves the refresh sleep real. `tests/test_asy_neopixel_driver.py` also takes 1.36 s instead of 0.77 s on the Unix port, because the port's poll costs 1 ms per scheduler round while a ThreadSafeFlag waits (`extmod/modselect.c:58, 353-369`), and the overlay now waits on its flag during a second queued ramp where it used to wait on the lock; rp2's poll has no such cost. Do you accept these frame-sequence changes for a cancel-safe overlay? The alternative keeps the old wake timing more closely by running the flag wait in its own task and having the overlay await an event that the wait's end or the alarm sets: one more task per wake, and still about one round faster than today.

New paragraph for F.2, after "One timeout mechanism":
> **`wait_for()`/`wait_for_ms()` lose a cancel that lands after their timeout fired** (v1.29.0; rp2 freezes this same `extmod/asyncio`, `ports/rp2/boards/manifest.py:2` with `extmod/asyncio/manifest.py:3-14`, and runs the same C `Task`, `extmod/asyncio/core.py:9`, `extmod/extmod.cmake:32`). Once its sleep ends the waiter cancels its runner and awaits it (`extmod/asyncio/funcs.py:48-51`). Awaiting a task makes that task the waiter's `data` (`extmod/modasyncio.c:299-303`), and `Task.cancel()` forwards a cancel to the task a task awaits (`:208-211`), so a cancel of the waiter landing before the runner ends goes to the awaitable, already cancelled; the runner then ends normally, the waiter no longer sleeping (`funcs.py:14-21`), and the waiter raises `TimeoutError` (`:51`): the cancel is lost. On the pinned Unix port a loop that catches that `TimeoutError` and goes on ran on when cancelled in that window, 5 trials of 5, and ended within 20 rounds when cancelled while the wait slept, 5 of 5. **Rule** (agent, 2026-10-28): a task a cancel can reach while it waits never times that wait with `wait_for()`/`wait_for_ms()` and goes on after its `TimeoutError`, whether a loop catches it or a caller reads a sentinel mapped from it and continues. Every supervised task is such a task: the shutdown sequence cancels and awaits each (A.8 step 4), and a lost cancel hangs that step until the watchdog resets with the command undone. Such a wait carries its own deadline instead, inside its own poll loop (`asy_udp_socket.py`'s `_poll()`, as the UART driver's `ready()`), or an alarm task that sets what the task awaits. `wait_for_ms()` stays where its `TimeoutError` ends the task's work (the webserver's waits: `_serve()` reclaims the connection and the task ends) or where no cancel reaches the wait in that window: the supervisor's escalation entry (the supervisor is cancelled only parked, A.8 step 1, and nothing on the device cancels `supervise_tasks()`) and the FRAM presence check (reached only inside a chunk read, which step 3's quiesce drains before step 4 cancels). An outer timeout's cancel landing in an inner wait's window is lost the same way, but the outer `wait_for()` still raises once its runner ends, so it stays bounded wherever the inner `TimeoutError` ends the awaited work, as for the webserver's per-call waits under the request cap. `tests/test_asy_udp_socket.py` and `tests/test_asy_ntp_client.py` cancel in each scheduler round after the deadline falls due; the `wait_for_ms()` form failed both.

## The UART hang found and fixed (agent, 2026-10-29, 14:59-15:20 UTC; commit 916fb878)

- **Cause:** `asy_uart_driver.py` stopped its receive ring with `DMA.active(False)` on the data channel, which is
  chained to the reload channel. rp2's `active(False)` is the SDK's `dma_channel_abort()`: it writes CHAN_ABORT, then
  spins in C until the channel is no longer busy. The abort completes the channel, the completion fires the chain, the
  reload restarts it, and the spin never ends; USB stops and the watchdog resets the board. Located with step markers in
  the watchdog's SCRATCH0 register (they survive the reset): the last step reached was `_rx_dma.active(False)` in
  `deinit()`. A probe confirmed it: aborting the data channel alone, or both channels together, leaves it busy;
  pointing its chain at itself first (`DMA.ctrl`, the non-triggering AL1_CTRL alias) stops both at once.
- **Reach in the product:** every `deinit()` or re-`init()` of a driver whose ring is armed (a task restart, a reconfig)
  hangs the firmware until the watchdog, reading as an unexplained watchdog reset. `rp2.DMA` is documented and not marked
  experimental (since v1.21), but its docs say nothing about aborting a chained channel.
- **Fix (bench branch; to carry to the audit branch):** `UART._rx_stop()` unchains, then aborts reload and data, used by
  `_arm_rx()` and `deinit()`. `tests/rp2.py`'s DMA fake now raises where silicon hangs (16 driver tests fail on the old
  driver); driver tests 174/174, comm tests 205/205, ruff clean; `UART_C_PORT_CHANGELOG.md` B93. mypy, the remaining
  tiers and the twin were not run.
- **Reruns with the fix, real watchdog:** all eight mounted UART scripts reached DONE.
  - idle poll rate: fast 1,233 / 1,227 rounds, idle 59 / 59 in 3 s (60 expected); `poll_idle_ms` works as designed.
  - recovery: the silence, desync and size-mismatch cases fail and recover, in 1.51 s (none) and 1.71-1.81 s (crc16)
    against the 7 s bound; log entry E81; no task exceptions.
  - exchange (none) and hazards (crc16) reached DONE; hazards' `stalled_tx`: failed, recovered after 2 attempts (E82).
    crc16 again shows `next_ok` false after a cancelled read mid-train and `clean_after` false after the cmd sweep.
  - break frame (none): it fails, the next frame succeeds.
- **DMA ring under interrupts off, its test fixed too:** the script started its reader only after the whole stream
  (window plus 50 ms either side, ~1.2 kB) had gone out, so the 512 B ring overran at every window and the host test's
  "all intact within the bound" could never pass. The reader now drains while the stream runs. CRC none: a 3 ms window
  keeps all 23 frames intact with no overrun; 45, 100 and 400 ms windows (beyond the 44.4 ms bound) overrun, are
  reported, and the 4 frames after each window all arrive. With the ring's DMA request off, the window sets UARTRSR.OE
  and the first frame fails, the next succeeds. CRC16: every window, 3 ms included, loses frames (2 intact) with an
  overrun: the CRC16 reader is slower than line rate on a back-to-back stream (cf. the CRC row, ~460 us/byte), so the
  ring fills regardless of the window. The protocol never streams frames back to back (each waits for its ACK), so this
  limits the test's stream, not the link.

## DMA across a soft reset, reset kinds, SPI, gc, sockets, DNS (bigq, 16:52-16:54 UTC)

Log: `logs/bigq_20261029.log`. Bare v1.29.0 image, scripts in `mp/big/`.

**The UART ring across a soft reset is clean.** With the driver's ring left running (channels 2 and 3, CYW43's PIO
channels 0 and 1 already taken), a soft reset left the ring channels neither busy nor enabled, the next claim got
channel 2 again, and after RXDMAE was re-enabled and 64 bytes sent no old channel counted a byte: all 64 stayed in the
UART FIFO. The finalizers (`gc_sweep_all` → `DMA.__del__` → `close()`) stop the chain since the B93 fix; no stray
channel writes into the new heap. With `deinit()` first: nothing left enabled, fresh claim gets channel 0. CYW43's two
channels (TREQ 8/12, PIO1) stay enabled across a soft reset, as expected for the radio driver.

**Reset kinds against `begin_boot()`.** Both `mem_backup` regions (4 + 3 words) survived all three kinds.

| kind | reset_cause() | WATCHDOG REASON | CHIP_RESET |
|---|---|---|---|
| `machine.reset()` | 3 (WDT_RESET) | 0x2 (FORCE) | 0x100 |
| soft reset | 3 (stale, from the previous hard reset) | 0x2 (stale) | 0x100 |
| watchdog timeout | 3 | 0x1 (TIMER) | 0x100 |

`reset_cause()` cannot tell `machine.reset()` from a watchdog timeout; WATCHDOG REASON can, and `begin_boot()`
records it raw. CHIP_RESET's HAD_POR (0x100) stays set after watchdog resets until the next power cycle, so a raw
`0x101`/`0x102` is a watchdog reset on a board that was powered on, not a power-on. A soft reset changes no hardware
register, so with no record and no boot-phase marker `begin_boot()` reports the previous hard cause (here: watchdog).
On `dev` a soft reset only comes from the console, so this mislabels only bench sessions; worth one line in Part A.7.

**SPI (FRAM, 100 reads of 16-1024 B per rate, against a 16-byte-chunk reference):** no mismatch and no EIO at 1, 8,
12 and 24 MHz. Requests round down: 20 MHz gives 12 MHz, 40 MHz gives 24 MHz (the ceiling). A read with all 12 free DMA
channels claimed is still correct. 100 reads took 220 ms at 1 MHz, 47 ms at 8 MHz, 35 ms at 24 MHz. `dev` runs FRAM
at the driver's default 1 MHz: a 1 KB synchronous transfer holds the loop about 8 ms there, about 1.5 ms at 8 MHz. A
cheap stall reduction for F.3, and a config pitfall (a 20 MHz setting silently runs at 12).

**gc.collect() pause against heap fill:** 9.6 ms at 25 %, 15.7 ms at 50 %, 21.8 ms at 75 %, 25.5 ms at 90 %
(1014-3614 small objects), linear at about 7 µs per live object. At 90 % that is over half of the 44 ms UART ring.

**lwIP pools from Python, station connected:** 4 UDP sockets (the 5th: ENOMEM), 9 TCP sockets, 8 listeners.
`MEMP_NUM_UDP_PCB` is `4 + LWIP_MDNS_RESPONDER`; DHCP client, mDNS and (in hotspot mode) the DHCP server take from the
same pool, so the hotspot's concurrent UDP users need counting (prims4 measures it with AP and STA up).

**getaddrinfo() (not used by `src/`; `asy_dns_client` resolves asynchronously):** 23 ms first, 1 ms cached, a numeric

## I2C faults injected on silicon (prims3 16:58, prims5 17:04 UTC)

Logs: `logs/prims34_20261029.log`, `logs/prims5_20261029.log`; scripts `mp/big/prims3.py`, `mp/big/prims5.py`. Bus 0
(GP13/GP12, 50 kHz, BMP3xx at 0x77). A stuck participant is injected by forcing the pad low through GPIOn_CTRL's
OEOVER/OUTOVER (RP2040 2.19.6.1), released by a hard one-shot Timer; low-only on an open-drain bus is electrically
safe. Any `Pin()` construction (and `gpio_set_function()`) rewrites GPIOn_CTRL and so drops the override, which
prims5 re-applies through a `Pin` stand-in for the driver's clear.

**A transfer never hangs on a held line; it returns at the configured timeout.** Measured on `readfrom_mem_into`:

| fault | timeout 50 ms | timeout 200 ms (bus 1's SCD30 setting) |
|---|---|---|
| SCL held before START for 2/20/45 ms | ok, call lasts the hold + 1 ms | ok |
| SCL held 60/150 ms | ETIMEDOUT after 50.9 ms | ok (61 / 151 ms) |
| SCL held 400/3000 ms | ETIMEDOUT after 50.9 ms | ETIMEDOUT after 200.9 ms |
| SCL pulled low mid-address-byte, 20 ms | ok | ok |
| same, 100 ms | ETIMEDOUT 50.9 ms | ok (101 ms) |
| same, 1000 ms | ETIMEDOUT 50.9 ms | ETIMEDOUT 200.9 ms |
| SDA pulled low mid-transfer for 5 ms | EIO after 5.8 ms | EIO after 5.8 ms |
| SDA stuck low | ETIMEDOUT 50.8 ms | — |

Every one of these is a synchronous call: the event loop is held for the whole duration. On bus 1 a stretching or
stuck SCD30 costs up to **201 ms of loop stall per failed transfer**, against the 44 ms UART ring; F.3's stall table
and the ring sizing should carry it.

**After a mid-transfer timeout the controller is wedged for exactly one more transfer.** The next transfer on the same
object fails again with ETIMEDOUT (another 50.7 ms); the one after succeeds (`retry_x3`: fail, ok, ok). The bus-clear
rung (`clear()`: bit-bang plus re-mux, no init) does not shorten it (its first transfer still fails), nor does
`scan()`; re-constructing the controller (the `recover()` rung) works at once. So the ladder converges at its second
transfer whatever the first rung is, at the cost of two timeouts (about 100 ms on bus 0, 400 ms on bus 1). A timeout
with SCL held from before START leaves no wedge.

**The driver's bus clear (`_clear_bus`, `_clear_bus_awaiting`) behaves as designed on silicon:** SDA stuck for good →
status 3 (SDA_LOW|SDA_STUCK) after 9 pulses and the STOP attempt, 3.4 ms; a slave letting go after k = 1-9 pulses →
status 1 after k+1 low phases; k = 10 → status 3; SCL stuck → status 4 after 50.7 ms (the boot-time sync clear blocks
for its timeout, which is fine at boot); the awaiting clear with SCL stuck → status 4 in 52 ms with at most 1 ms loop
lag. After a clear the pins are SIO: the old controller object fails (ETIMEDOUT) until the pins are re-muxed, and the
re-mux alone brings it back on a free bus, so `clear()`'s re-mux without init is sound.

**`scan()` is a software-I2C routine on rp2.** Zero-length writes go through `mp_machine_soft_i2c_transfer`
(`ports/rp2/machine_i2c.c:127-144`) and `gpio_set_function()` re-selects I2C afterwards. A scan therefore clocks the bus
as GPIO, which is why the morning's "the first hardware scan frees a held SDA" happened: a scan is an accidental bus
clear. It also succeeds on a bus whose hardware controller is wedged (it found 0x18 and 0x77 there).

## The DMA/IRQ users together, and a UDP flood (prims4 17:10, prims6 17:14-17:19 UTC)

Logs: `logs/prims5b_20261029.log` (prims4 rerun), `logs/prims6_20261029.log`, `logs/uart_hang_{1,2}.log`; scripts
`mp/big/prims4.py`, `mp/big/prims6.py`, `mp/big/uart_hang{,2,3}.py`. Progress markers in `mem_backup()` are read back
after each run, before any hard reset, with the watchdog REASON.

**The UART ring, SPI DMA and CYW43 coexist without corruption.** 45 s quiet, all at once (UART0 → UART1 64-byte
frames every 8 ms into the driver's 512-byte ring, 1 KB FRAM reads at 8 MHz every 5 ms, a UDP echo over CYW43, a
10 ms lag probe): 4170/4171 frames intact, no sequence gap, no overrun; 5951 FRAM reads, none wrong; loop lag at most
17 ms. Then under the flood: 1530/1530 frames, 2136 FRAM reads, none wrong. Without Wi-Fi: the same (4375 frames,
6014 reads). Without FRAM traffic (UART ring beside a connected CYW43, 17:56, `logs/prims5c_20261029.log`): 4589/4590
frames, no gap or overrun, lag at most 15 ms; without the UART: 6640 FRAM reads, none wrong, lag at most 45 ms. Limitation: the UART sender is a task in the same loop, so it pauses whenever the loop stalls and the
ring is never stressed by the stalls themselves; a sender outside the loop (PIO, or the C peer) is owed for that.

**A UDP flood on the LAN stalls the whole event loop for seconds, below Python, and can reach the watchdog.** With the
echo yielding after every packet, a host flood (3 s at 200 pps, then 12 s unthrottled, 200-byte datagrams to a bound
port) held the loop for **5.9 s** in one stretch while Python saw only 93 packets; prims4's flood gave 6.1 s; one
prims6 run stalled 7.97 s and the **watchdog reset the board** (REASON TIMER, last feed one second into the flood).
The CPU goes to CYW43/lwIP receive processing, which Wi-Fi can feed faster than the RP2040 can drop packets. Echo
rates before saturation: 200 pps 2599/2600 echoed, 1000 pps 8723/13001, unthrottled 152/83572. No Python-level
mitigation exists (the work never reaches the loop); the watchdog is the backstop, and F.3/F.2 should state that LAN
receive load is an unbounded stall source. An unthrottled flood from the first packet never reached the board at all
(ARP/AP), so the stall needs a flood that ramps up.

**The hotspot leaves Python 2 UDP sockets.** With the AP interface up beside a connected station, the third
`socket(AF_INET, SOCK_DGRAM)` fails with ENOMEM (station alone: 4). `MEMP_NUM_UDP_PCB` is `4 + LWIP_MDNS_RESPONDER`;
the DHCP server and the station's DHCP client take theirs. Captive DNS plus one DNS or NTP client socket fit; a third
concurrent UDP user in hotspot mode does not. AP up takes 22 ms when the station is connected (99 ms when not), down
157 ms.

**A probe mistake worth a guard: `UART.read()` without the session lock never yields.** prims4's first runs reset the
board: its reader called the driver's `read()` without holding `session_lock`, `_active_uart()` returned None, and
`read()` returned None before reaching `ready()`'s yield, so the retry loop spun the event loop until the watchdog
(16,000 reads in 8 s). `UARTComm` always holds the lock, so the firmware is not affected, but CLAUDE.md's "the yield
lives in `ready()`, which every read loop goes through" does not hold on the no-session early return; a `sleep_ms(0)`
there (or a test pinning that callers hold the lock) would make the invariant unconditional. The ring's overrun latch
itself clears as designed (`uart_hang3.py`: pending set on the lap, cleared by the next `_buffered()`, level 0 after).

## Can a UDP storm be handled without blocking the loop? (prims7/prims8, 17:26-17:38 UTC)

Logs: `logs/prims7_20261029.log`, `logs/prims8_20261029.log`; scripts `mp/big/prims7.py`, `mp/big/prims8.py`. Storm:
3 s at 200 pps, then 8 s unthrottled, 200-byte datagrams; a hard 1 kHz Timer, a soft 100 Hz Timer and a 10 ms loop-lag
probe counted per second; afterwards 20 paced echoes on port 5005 for the RTT. Radio power save stays off, as the
firmware sets it (`_PM_NO_POWERSAVE`); power-save modes are not an option (owner, 2026-10-29: "Power save mode is not
an option"), so no result for them is recorded.

**Where the time goes.** During the stall the hard Timer keeps exactly 1000 ticks/s while the soft Timer drops to
8-12/s: the CPU is in the CYW43 PendSV dispatch (`mpnetworkport.c:95`, `cyw43_poll`), whose packet loop
(`cyw43_ll.c:1128`, `for (;;)` until no frame is left) never ends while frames keep arriving. That is below the
interpreter and above thread mode, so nothing in Python, the scheduler or soft Timers runs.

**The socket side does not matter.** Bound port with a yielding echo: stall up to 2.9 s; the storm on an unbound port
(nothing listening, lwIP drops or answers it): up to 3.1 s with zero packets reaching Python. The cost is per frame in
the bus transfer and lwIP input, before any socket.

**The chip's own packet filter removes the stall for a port the firmware does not serve.** `WLAN.ioctl(263 << 1 | 1,
iovar)` reaches the CYW43 firmware (WLC_SET_VAR; `cyw43_ll_ioctl` takes `cmd >> 1` and the low bit as set; the driver
discards the return code, so only the effect shows). A pattern filter (`pkt_filter_add`: id, type 0, negate,
offset 36 = UDP destination port behind a 20-byte IPv4 header, 2 bytes, mask ffff, pattern 0x138e) plus
`pkt_filter_enable`:

| filter | storm to 5006 | worst loop lag | soft Timer | paced echo on 5005 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (prims7) | 149,069 sent | 3.1 s | 8/s | 20/20, 6.3 ms |
| match 5006, negate 0 | 43,857 sent | 2.5 s | 9/s | **0/20** |
| match 5006, negate 1 | 153,588 sent | **5 ms** | 100/s | 20/20, 6.1 ms |

So in the default filter mode an enabled filter turns the chip into forward-on-match: only frames some filter matches
reach the host (negate 0 forwarded only 5006 and cut everything else); with negate 1 everything except 5006 is
forwarded and the storm dies in the chip. (The `pkt_filter_mode 1` case did not connect and gave no result.) Filters
live in chip RAM, so they are gone after `active(False)`, `deinit()` or a reset and would be re-installed at each
activation.

**What this means for the firmware.** An allowlist in the chip (ARP, the DHCP client and, in hotspot mode, server,
DNS and NTP replies by source port, TCP 80, captive DNS on UDP 53, mDNS if used) would make storms on every other
port harmless, which covers scans, stray broadcast/multicast storms and misdirected traffic. A storm aimed at an
allowed port (TCP 80, UDP 53, ARP) still reaches the RP2040 and still stalls the loop; only C can bound that, by giving
`cyw43_poll` a per-dispatch frame budget and re-arming the host-wake IRQ after a short delay when the budget runs out,
so thread mode keeps a guaranteed share and the chip drops the excess. That would be a new patch in
`toolchain/micropython_overrides.py`'s mechanism (B.14) and needs a reflash to test. Both are open design questions
for the owner.

**Decision on the storm (owner, 2026-10-29).** Asked whether to pursue the chip filter (reached only through the
undocumented `WLAN.ioctl`) or a budgeted `cyw43_poll`: "C changes are clearly out of scope." and "If there is no
official Micropython way to handle it, we'll have to accept it as is. No patching or tinkering." So a LAN storm that
stalls the loop stays an accepted limitation with the hardware watchdog as its backstop; neither the filter nor a C
patch is adopted. What remains to record is the fact itself: SPECIFICATION.md F.3's stall sources should name LAN
receive load as unbounded (measured up to 7.97 s, one watchdog reset), and F.2 the watchdog as its only recovery.

## UART receive line faults and GPIO interrupt storms (prims9, prims10, 18:13-18:15 UTC)

Log: `logs/prims910_20261029.log` (the first block, 18:10-18:12, is invalid: a probe bug in prims9 and a wrong
injection in prims10, both explained below); scripts `mp/big/prims9.py`, `mp/big/prims10.py`, runner `prims910.sh`.

**A held-low or glitching receive line never stalls the loop, and the link recovers with the next frame.** The UART1
receive pad (GPIO9, behind the driver's 512-byte DMA ring) was forced low through IO_BANK0 INOVER while UART0 sent a
64-byte frame every 8 ms. Held low for 1 ms, 100 ms and 10 s, the first intact frame came 17 ms after release (the next
frame's slot), loop lag stayed at the baseline (at most 14 ms against 16 ms quiet), and a 10 s hold only made the
reader's 200 ms reads time out (50 of them). Glitches for 2 s (a ~1-bit pulse at 1 kHz, or a 2 kHz square wave of 250 us
lows, i.e. a break every 500 us): lag at most 16 ms, the first intact frame 21-26 ms after the glitching stopped.

**The driver's UARTRSR check sees a line error only when the line goes quiet after it.** UARTRSR read 0x5 (FE and BE)
at the end of the 1 ms hold, yet the driver counted nothing: the next frame's characters reached the DMA before the
reader's next round, and RSR's FE and BE describe the last character read from UARTDR. During the 100 ms and 10 s holds
the reader polled while the line sat low and counted it (one each). Under the 1-bit glitches the driver flagged 8
errors while 134 frames arrived corrupted (10318 bytes skipped by resync); under the 250 us glitches it flagged 574.
So a mid-stream error is caught only by a CRC or the frame length, as the crossover flash test's comment states, and
`src/asy_uart_driver.py:80` ("OE, FE and BE each fail the frame") overstates it. `dev`'s two links run the default
`crc = "none"` (`devices/dev.toml`), so a corrupted payload byte of the right length passes the driver there.

**IO_BANK0 INOVER does not reach the GPIO edge detector.** Forcing GPIO6's input high and low changes `Pin.value()`, but
INTR0 kept the raw pad's state and no callback ran (four forced edges, 0 callbacks; the first prims10 block counted 0
callbacks at every rate on GPIO6 and GPIO11). An interrupt cannot be injected that way; the storm below toggles a
floating pin's pad pulls instead (GPIO22, verified floating: reads 1 with the pull-up, 0 with the pull-down; nothing is
driven).

**A GPIO interrupt storm is absorbed up to 5000 edges/s and starves the scheduler at 10000.** The handlers had the
drivers' shapes (ISL29125's bound `_on_irq`, an attribute store and `ThreadSafeFlag.set()`, on falling edges; SCD30's
lambda calling `set()`, on rising), soft as in the firmware, with a 100 Hz soft Timer and a 10 ms lag probe beside them,
5 s per rate:

| Edges/s | Callbacks / edges | Soft Timer (of 500) | Loop lag max | Lag samples (of ~550) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50, 500 | all | 500 | 1-4 ms | 547-550 |
| 2500 | 12486 / 12500 | 498-500 | 2-3 ms | 512-515 |
| 5000 | 24907-24968 / 25000 | 498-500 | 8-30 ms | 441-481 |
| 10000 | 8284-8555 / 50000 | **18-96** | **481-1223 ms** | 59-87 |
| 10000, no IRQ attached (control) | - | 500 | 2 ms | 491 |

The control shows the 20 kHz hard timer making the edges is not the load. At 10000 edges/s the scheduler queue (depth
8) is full: five of six pin callbacks and most soft-Timer callbacks are dropped, which in the firmware means sensor
trigger timers lost (Part F's soft-Timer-callback-drop), and the loop runs about every 85 ms. The watchdog was still fed
in 5 s. Flag wakeups coalesce from 2500 edges/s on (4364 of 12486), which the drivers' event loops expect. Where such a
storm could come from on `dev`: GPIO11 (SCD30 RDY) is push-pull and idles high; GPIO6 (ISL29125 INT, open-drain) read 0
at idle on this bare image, so its pull-up path was not exercised; a floating INT line (pull-up lost) is the case.

**Decision on interrupt storms (owner, 2026-10-29).** Shown the table above, the owner answered: "Interrupt storms are
not really an issue. That only can happen on a board which is already defective." No mitigation is planned; the
finding stays as the measured boundary.

## Clients that vanish mid-request (prims11, 18:31-18:41 UTC)

Log: `logs/prims11_20261029.log`; scripts `mp/big/prims11.py` (board), `prims11_host.py` (host), runner `prims11.sh`.
The board ran an HTTP server shaped like the webserver's hardening: `asyncio.start_server`, backlog 5, every read and
drain bounded by 5 s (the firmware's `web.per_call_timeout_s`), close on any failure. The bench host emulated clients
that leave the network without a word: scapy on `br0`, each client an unused LAN address (ARP-checked, 51 of 54 free)
with a locally administered MAC. Each opened TCP to the board and then answered nothing, ARP included. Interface and
bridge configuration were not touched. A real kernel client fetched a page every 2 s throughout. Caveat: the bare image
carries upstream modlwip, not the firmware's `modlwip_eagain` patches (B.14.4).

**A vanished client is reclaimed in about 15 s and leaks nothing.** Six clients sent half a request head and vanished:
each read timed out at 5 s, the close sent a FIN, and modlwip aborted the connection with a RST 10 s later
(`MICROPY_PY_LWIP_TCP_CLOSE_TIMEOUT_MS` = 10000, `extmod/modlwip.c:64`, v1.29.0); the last packet to each came
14.5-14.7 s after it vanished. The same held for nine clients that sent a full request for a 16 KB response and then
acknowledged nothing (two data segments, then FIN and RST, the last at 14.5-14.9 s). Every handler ended (`open` back to 0, 15
timeouts for 15 clients), lwIP gave 9 TCP sockets again at every later check, and the real client was served
throughout the half-head phase (60 of 60).

**Ten at once briefly exhaust the TCP pool.** Opening the ten full-request clients within a second hit the pool: the
tenth got no SYN-ACK, and two real-client fetches timed out (t 147-153) until the first timeouts fired. Recovery was
on its own; the fetches before and after succeeded.

**Half-open connections fill the listen backlog for about 12 s.** Eight clients sent only a SYN: five got SYN-ACKs
(retransmitted once, the last 8.5-9.0 s later), three got nothing (backlog 5 full), and three real-client fetches timed
out between t 303 and 315. A phone that drops off between SYN and ACK is rare; this is the same ceiling the backlog sets
for any burst of new connections.

## Real flash writes beside the UART ring, FRAM and Wi-Fi (prims12, 18:56-18:57 UTC)

Run once on the owner's instruction (owner, 2026-10-29, asked whether the real flash-write stall test was still worth
running: "Try that flash write test once"). Log: `logs/prims12_20261029.log`; scripts `mp/big/prims12.py` (board),
`prims12_host.py` (host UDP echo every 10 ms), runner `prims12.sh`. One scratch file, `/u35_flashprobe.json` (about
700 B, above littlefs's 512 B inline limit at `progsize=256`, so each write takes its own data block), was written three
times in the shape of a config save (one `json.dump()` straight into the file) and then removed; a check after the
reset found it gone. Wear spent: three file writes and one remove, at most a handful of 4 KB sector erases. The real
config files on the board are 16-205 B, all inline, so a real config save is usually lighter than this probe.
Interrupts-off windows were timed by a 5 kHz hard Timer (a gap over 2 ms is recorded). UART0 sent a 16 KB stream of
back-to-back frames paced by DMA (`TXDMAE`), so it kept sending while interrupts were off, unlike every earlier
self-fed UART test.

| Phase | Loop blocked by the write | Interrupts-off windows over 2 ms | Loop lag max | Wi-Fi echoes lost | FRAM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline (no write) | - | none (longest gap 0.23 ms) | 34 ms | 0 | 136 reads, 0 bad |
| write 1 (creates the file) | 207 ms | 37.5, 35.7, 2.3 ms | 214 ms | 18 (about 180 ms) | 0 bad |
| write 2 (rewrite) | 70 ms | 35.3 ms | 78 ms | 4 | 0 bad |
| write 3 (rewrite) | 76 ms | 39.4 ms | 85 ms | 4 | 0 bad |
| remove | 4.4 ms | none (longest 0.56 ms) | - | - | - |

- **Each sector erase holds interrupts off for 35-39 ms**, one per rewrite and two on the first create. Page programs
  stay under 2 ms. 39.4 ms is below the receive ring's 44.4 ms line-rate bound (512 B at 115200 baud), and the ring
  is DMA-fed, so a frame in flight is not lost to the window. The flash chip's datasheet is not in `datasheets/`, so
  the worst-case erase time could not be checked; the earlier 45/400 ms windows (H86) remain the assumption for it.
- **The loop, not the interrupts, is the longer stall:** 70-207 ms per write, the whole `open()`/`json.dump()`/`close()`
  running synchronously. This is a row for F.3's stall table. The UART link's agreed timeout is 1000 ms, and the
  protocol never streams frames back to back, so one frame waiting in the ring survives a stall of this length.
- **Wi-Fi survives; UDP arriving during the stall is dropped, not queued.** No disconnect. The echoes sent while the
  loop was blocked were lost (18, 4, 4 of 100 per second); those sent just before came back 55-213 ms late. This is
  modlwip's UDP receive queue overflowing while the loop doesn't run. Any loop stall of this length does the same.
  A DNS client retries.
- **The UART stream part of this run is not evidence.** The Python reader could not keep up with a back-to-back
  line-rate stream alongside the 5 kHz timer, Wi-Fi and FRAM, and overran 8-9 times per stream in the baseline as well
  as in the write phases. The ring result above therefore rests on the measured window length, not on frame counts.
- The reset recorded after the run was the watchdog timer (`wd_reason` 0x1): the script armed it and ended at DONE,
  so it fired 8 s later. It was not a crash; the markers show the run's last state (longest gap 39,379 us).
